INTERNATIONAL SPACE STATION

The International Space Station (ISS) is a space station, or a habitable artificial satellite, in low Earth orbit. It is a multinational collaborative project involving five participating space agencies: NASA (United States), Roscosmos (Russia), JAXA (Japan), ESA (Europe), and CSA (Canada). The ISS serves as a laboratory for scientific research, technology development, and international cooperation in space exploration. It has been continuously inhabited since November 2000 and provides valuable insights into the effects of long-duration spaceflight on the human body, as well as opportunities for conducting experiments in various scientific fields. The ISS orbits the Earth approximately every 90 minutes, allowing astronauts to experience multiple sunrises and sunsets each day.

Key Features of the ISS

  • Orbiting Earth at an average altitude of approximately 408 kilometers (253 miles).
  • Supports a crew of six astronauts and cosmonauts.
  • Equipped with laboratories for research in biology, physics, astronomy, and other sciences.
  • Provides a platform for testing spacecraft systems and equipment in microgravity.
  • Facilitates international collaboration and cultural exchange among participating nations.
